Why It's Built This Way

The discipline is the edge—so we structure the whole firm around it.

Tidebreak profits from being early on public policy, never from non-public government information. That single rule only holds if the firm is designed to make it unavoidable.

So the entities that touch policy directly—advocacy and consulting—are legally and operationally separate from Tidebreak Capital. Information moves outward to the public record, never inward as a private sourcing channel. The barrier isn't a disclaimer; it's the architecture.

How The Walls Work

Compliance as moat, in practice.

Separate by design

Distinct legal entities, systems, and personnel policies. Tidebreak Capital and the policy-facing entities do not share a private information pipeline.

One direction only

Perspective flows out to the public record. Nothing non-public flows back into Tidebreak's investment process.

Documented & auditable

Positions trace to public sources. The trail is the proof—and the barrier to entry for anyone unwilling to hold the same line.

Common Questions

For prospective partners.

What does Tidebreak actually invest in? +
Concentrated positions in Life Sciences businesses, sized to public policy catalysts we believe the broader market has mispriced. Tidebreak Capital acts on publicly available information only.
How is Tidebreak's edge not just inside information? +
The opposite. Our edge is being early and correct on public policy—reading the seam between the science and the politics better than a generalist or a pure clinical specialist. Information barriers exist specifically so Tidebreak never relies on non-public government information.
Why are there three entities? +
Because the discipline only holds if it's built into the architecture. The advocacy and consulting entities are legally and operationally separate from Tidebreak Capital and are not a sourcing channel for it.
Who can invest? +
